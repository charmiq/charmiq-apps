# How It Works

Stop Motion makes stop-motion films of toy-brick scenes. Each Application does one job: [Brick Studio](charmiq://../brick-studio/README.md) is the screen, where the film is seen on its set; Stop Motion is the timeline, which decides what moment is shown and when, and exports the film; a code editor's `scene.js` tab holds the scene. They find each other in the document and talk through the Platform's commands.


## The Scene

The scene is JavaScript. Stop Motion runs it whenever it changes, in a worker of its own: the code reaches no page, no document and no other Application, only the commands below, and a scene that runs for more than four seconds is stopped. A mistake is shown with its line, and the last film that ran stays on screen meanwhile.

Positions are Brick Studio's: `x` east and `z` north in studs, `height` up in plates (a brick is 3 plates), fractions allowed. Times are seconds.

| Command | Does |
|---|---|
| `scene({ title, fps, length, width, height, floor, boil, light, drawn })` | the film's title, frames a second (24), length in seconds (the last key's when left out), its export size (1280 × 720), the area (`{ x, z, width, depth }` in studs) the studio's baseplate covers at least, so actors have ground to move over, its `boil` (0 to 1, none when left out): every actor not kept still shifted a little from frame to frame, as bricks moved by hand between exposures, and its `light` over the look's own (`{ key, fill, exposure, soft }`: the key light scaled 0 to 3, the fill 0 to 4, the exposure 0.25 to 4, the key's shadows softened 0 to 1; each its look's own when left out), as Brick Studio's stage takes it, its `sky` over every set's own (`{ horizon, zenith, clouds, seed }` or `{ image }`, with `lights`, as Brick Studio's `setSky` takes it: a film's sky, each set's own when left out), and how it is `drawn`: `'twos'` (when left out) each drawing held two frames, as a brick film is shot, counted afresh from each cut (a camera's or a set's), so a cut lands on its own frame, a walker drawn in its gait's own drawings, each a pose at its keys; `'ones'` a new drawing every frame; `'smooth'` moving on every frame between keys. An actor or a camera drawn otherwise says so: `a.drawn('smooth')`, `camera.drawn('ones')` |
| `actor(name, source)` | an actor: a component of the studio's build (`{ component: 'car' }`), a catalog part dressed as a placement is (`{ part: 'figure', color: 'blue', legColor, skin, face, hat, rightHand, leftHand }`, `skin` its head and hands, yellow when left out: light-gray in a black-and-white film), or a piece already standing in the build, by where `listPlacedParts` says it stands (`{ placed: { x: 30, z: -20, level: 0 } }`, and `set: 'Garage'` for a set other than the first), or one part of a copy of a component standing there, by where it stands in the component as Brick Studio's `listComponents({ component })` lists its parts (`{ placed: { x: 30, z: -20, level: 0, inside: { x: 1, z: 2, level: 1 } } }`: the clerk of one store, the store left standing): it is lifted out of the set, dressed and posed as it is there; each value the scene keys comes from its keys from the first frame (held before its first key, as for any actor), and every value it never keys from where the piece stood |
| `a.at(time, values, ease)` | a key: the actor's values at a time, eased into from the key before (`linear`, `ease-in`, `ease-out`, `ease-in-out`, the default, or `hold`); a figure's `face`, `rightHand` and `leftHand` (`{ part, color, grip }` as Brick Studio dresses a hand, or `null` for nothing) are taken at once and held, a coffee picked up partway through |
| `a.path(from, to, (u, t) => values)` | values from a function over a span (`u` from 0 to 1 across it, `t` in seconds), in place of the keys there. A path keys nothing outside its span: before it, a channel it eases reads its keys, so a channel first keyed after the path (a flight's pitch keyed at its landing) stays at that key's value from the film's start until the path begins, the figure lying down all through its run-up. Key the channel's rest value at 0 and just before the path (`a.at(0, { pitch: 0 })`, `a.at(from - 0.05, { pitch: 0 })`): keyed at 0 alone, it eases toward the later key over the whole wait |
| `a.wobble(value, amount, { rate, from, to })` | smooth noise added to one value: `amount` at most, `rate` swings a second |
| `a.ride(other, { x, z, height })` | stands at an offset in another actor's frame and turns with it; its own position and turn add to the ride |
| `a.walk(time, points, { speed, until, gait, stride, arms, variance, bounce, lean, seed })` | walks along `points` (each `{ x, z }`) from `time` at `speed` studs a second (3), or arriving `until` a time, facing the way it goes: a figure or a creature on studs a stud a step, turning a quarter on one foot and hopping up and down a level (see below); anything else keyed through each point, turning at each corner, its legs and arms swinging in step with the ground it covers, a bounce and a lean. Its arms, lean and leading foot are its own (seeded by its name, or `seed`); on studs it walks the scene's gait `gait` names (below; the built-in walk when it names none), its arms swinging as far as that gait's `arms` (38° in the built-in) about where its `hang` hangs them, unless the walk names its own `arms`, and `variance` (0 to 3, 1 when not given) sets how far each step wanders, as a hand-animated walk does; `stride` and `bounce` move only an actor that does not click onto studs |
| `a.idle({ from, to, energy, nervous, seed })` | alive while it stands: breathing, its weight shifting, small turns, glances held a moment; livelier with `energy` and quicker and wider with `nervous` (each 0 to 1). A figure idles subtly on its own wherever nothing else moves it (see below) |
| `a.still()` | stands as keyed alone: no idling of its own, and the scene's boil leaves it |
| `a.look(target, { from, to, within, max })` | turns its head toward an actor or a point while it stands within `within` studs (12), as far as `max` degrees (70) either way of where its torso faces, easing there and back; of two looks at once, the one begun latest |
| `a.react(time, face, { for })` | a face for `for` seconds (1) over whatever it wears, then its own again |
| `a.ground({ step, start })` | its height follows the ground under it: what it is keyed is then plates above the surface it stands on, stepping up onto one at most `step` plates (3, a brick) above where it stood and down onto any below; before a surface higher its height holds, as at a wall, though its keys still move it on (ground sets heights; a walk leaps a low part in its way, see below, and walks into anything else); every part's top is a surface, a high-jump bar's as much as a floor's, so a grounded figure steps up onto a bar it reaches within `step`, under any part of its footprint, not only its middle: a jumper grounded with `step: 12` flying over a bar 8 plates above the mat has its keys counted from the bar's top while its footprint overlaps it, and pops up by the bar's height. Keep `step` below a bar's rise over what the figure takes off from and lands on (over a 6-plate mat, a bar at 14: `step` under 8) (`describeFilm`'s footing names the part the ground stands a figure on, `on`, where its footing goes wrong); where it is first shown, and shown again after being hidden, it starts afresh: on the floor, or with `start` on the surface nearest that level (a gallery: `start: 24`) |
| `gait(name, { ... })` | a gait for the scene's walks on studs: the keys it changes from the built-in walk, each in the built-in's form (`steps.ts` `WALK`: curves keyed at a move's drawings, numbers, and its `timing`, each kind of move's drawings against a step's). A walk takes it by `gait: name`; named `walk`, it is every walk's that names none. The Gait mode shapes one on its graphs and writes it here |
| `crowd(name, { along, count, width, personalities, wardrobe, stops, react, go, from, to, seed })` | walkers named `name-1` on, walking `along` a path both ways over its `width` (4 studs), dressed from the wardrobe (`colors`, `legColors`, `skins`, `faces`, `hats`, `hairColors`; no `skins`, yellow heads and hands; `skins: ['light-gray']` for a black-and-white film), each a personality: `commuter`, `window-shopper` (stops at `stops`, turning to face each), `tourist` (pauses looking up), `phone` (a radio held up), `jogger`, `stroller`, `couple` (in pairs, talking), `waiter` (waits until `go`). Each is already under way when the crowd starts, out of step with the others, each step lasting as long as its pace takes to cover a stud. They keep right, slow behind a slower walker, pass when the other side is clear and wait when not, keeping about a body apart along the path and side by side (spacing on the path, not collisions with the set or other actors); `react: { at, where, within, look, for }` stops those near what happens, the nearest first, staring and surprised. It answers the walkers, to key further |
| `camera.at(time, { position \| orbit, target, fov, shift, avoid }, ease)` | the camera: standing at a point, or orbiting its target (`{ angle, distance, height }`: angle 0 south of it, 90 east, round counterclockwise seen from above; with `relativeTo: 'heading'`, round the actor it looks at as it turns: 0 behind it, 90 its right, 180 before it), looking at a point or an actor (`target: car`, or `{ actor: car, height }` to look higher), an actor followed as a camera operator follows one: the actor moves within the picture and the camera follows only what lasts, its height and its place over about a second (a leap, a hop or a step's bob moves nothing and a step's sway or a leap's lurch is left out, while a climb or a staircase still tilts it and a run still pans, a walk's corner cut by about a third of a stud), as far ahead as behind, so a steady move is never lagged, and never across a cut, a set change or a moment the actor is hidden; `{ actor: ball, lock: true }` aims at its exact place every frame instead, for something the shot must track tightly, such as a thrown ball (an orbit circles the aim either way, so it rides the jumps only when locked; `focus` on an actor stays on its exact place), through a lens of `fov` degrees (32), turned clockwise about where it looks by `roll` degrees as seen from behind it, its lens shifted up by `shift`, a share of the picture's height from -1 to 1 (down when negative: a level camera at `shift: -0.37` looks down at the floor, its horizon 0.13 down the picture, every vertical still upright where pitching the camera down would lean them; Blender's vertical lens shift, though Blender's `shift_y` is a share of the picture's larger side, so a landscape film's `shift` is `shift_y` times its width over its height), sharp at its `focus` (an actor, `{ actor, height }`, a point, or a number of studs ahead along its view; its target when no key names one) through its `aperture` (an f-number from 1 to 32: 1.4 a brick film's shallow macro look, only what it focuses on sharp; 8 a toy photo's; 16 nearly all sharp), each eased between keys like any value, so a key's new focus is a rack focus (see *The edit*); `dollyZoom: true` on a lens key keeps the target its size on the screen while the lens eases into the key (see *The edit*); `avoid: 'parts'` keeps it out of the set's parts, drawn in toward its target until it stands clear and sees it, from this key to the next placing it (a view's camera too) |
| `camera.cut(time, { position \| orbit, target, fov, roll }, name, { transition, seconds })` | a cut: the camera jumps to these values at `time`, starting a new shot (a followed actor is followed afresh from it), named for the timeline and storyboards; the film's camera's cut may enter by a `transition` over `seconds` (0.5, up to 10): `dissolve`, `wipe-left`, `wipe-right`, `wipe-up`, `wipe-down` (the edge moving that way) or `iris` (opening from the middle) |
| `camera.shake(from, to, amount, rate)` | a handheld shake |
| `view(name)` | another camera, used the same way: a second Brick Studio in the document shows the scene through it |
| `view(name, { plan: true })` | a plan: the scene from straight above, north up, with every actor's path and the camera drawn over it. It takes no keys; `{ plan: { x, z, width, depth } }` frames an area instead of everything the film shows |
| `set.at(time, name)` | a cut to another of the build's sets (Brick Studio's [sets](charmiq://../brick-studio/How%20It%20Works.md)): the set of that name stands from `time` on, until the next; the build's first set before the first |
| `fade.at(time, value, ease, { color, shape })`, `fade.out(from, to, { color })`, `fade.in(from, to, { color })` | the fade: 0 shows the frame, 1 is its color (`#rrggbb`, black when left out), over the whole picture or, with `shape: 'iris'`, closing in from the frame's edge; each fade takes the color and shape of the key it eases into |
| `fade.flash(time, { color, hold, out })`, `fade.irisOut(from, to, { color })`, `fade.irisIn(from, to, { color })` | a flash: the frame all `color` (white) at `time`, held `hold` seconds (0.04), back over `out` (0.3); an iris closing on the frame, and opening on it |
| `speed.at(time, rate, ease)`, `speed.slow(from, to, rate, { ease })` | how fast the scene runs on the screen from a key: 1 as it is, 0.25 a quarter as fast, 2 twice (0.05 to 8), eased into from the key before; `slow` runs a span at `rate` (0.25), easing in and out over `ease` seconds (0.2; 0 at once) |
| `title(from, to, text, { style })` | words over the film: a full `card`, or a `caption` along the foot |
| `sound.at(time, sound, { volume, until, fadeIn, fadeOut })` | a sound from `time`: a file in the Platform by its URI (`charmiq://file/<assetId>`), or one Stop Motion ships by name; once, or over and over `until` a time; `volume` from 0 to 4 (1 as recorded), rising from silence over `fadeIn` seconds and falling to silence over `fadeOut` before it ends (two overlapping sounds' fades cross them over) |
| `a.sound(time, sound, { volume, until, fadeIn, fadeOut })` | a sound that follows an actor: it sounds from the side of the picture the actor stands on, quieter as it stands farther than 20 studs off or behind the camera |
| `a.footsteps(sounds, { volume, from, to })` | a footstep wherever a figure sets a foot down as it steps (a step's heel, a hop's landing, a turn's free foot), as the film lays its steps, on studs or off: one of `sounds` (a name, or a list it takes turns at, never the same twice running) at `volume`, from `from` until `to` (the film's end), following it as `a.sound` does. A figure that lays no steps (one riding, or in the air) makes none |
| `arc(t, up, gravity)`, `noise(t, seed)`, `lerp(a, b, u)` | a jump's height in plates after `t` seconds, smooth noise from -1 to 1 (a seed its own run of it), and a blend |
| `random(seed)` | a random generator, from 0 up to 1: the same numbers for the same seed on every run. `Math.random` is seeded by the scene, so a film is the same every run |
| `console.log(...)` | a line in the film's log, beside the cast |

An actor's values are `x`, `z`, `height`, `yaw` (clockwise seen from above; a figure faces south at 0, so 180 faces north), `pitch` (raising its south side, which is a figure's front), `roll` (raising its west side), `visible` (shown or hidden from its key on, at once, until the next: a key hiding an actor at 0 and one showing it at 87 shows it from 87), and a figure's `pose`: `head`, `leftArm`, `rightArm`, `leftLeg`, `rightLeg`, and on film alone `twist` (its upper body turning over its hips, clockwise seen from above), `lean` (its upper body leaning forward over its hips), `rightWrist` and `leftWrist` (each hand turning on its forearm), all at any angle. A value eases from its key to the next by the numbers keyed, the long way round when they say so: a pitch eased from -270 to 0 sweeps through -180, head down; key -360 to come upright the short way. A figure's `face` is a name (`listParts` in Brick Studio names them, and `frameFaces` the ones a film alone wears: `scared` and `asleep`): it changes at its key, at once, and stays until the next, the face the actor was declared with showing before the first. A face never eases from one to another; the swap is the stop-motion way, and it reads best on a motion that hides it (a head turn, a shake, arms flying up). Its position is the middle of the bottom of its footprint, and it turns about that point. A figure's height is its lowest point, as a part's level is in a build: its soles as it stands, its hips seated (both legs at 90), its soles then 2.4 plates below them, so a sitter is keyed at its seat's top and a ridden figure sits where its offset puts its seat.

An actor with wheels rolls them on its own: they turn by the distance it moves along its own north.

A figure or a creature on studs is clicked onto them, as in a brick film: its feet on two studs side by side, square to them, never sliding. A walk is a stud a step, as a rendered brick film walks: the body glides over the edge between two rows of studs without holding, the trailing toe on that edge until three quarters through the step, the leading leg reaching out and landing on its heel there halfway, the hips riding where the feet put them, and the next row clicks in; a corner is a quarter turn on the foot inside the turn, the body leaning out over that foot, rolled onto its outer edge as a palm rolls over a fist (a turn on the foot without the weight starts with a shift of weight onto it); a diagonal is a staircase of steps and turns; a level up to two plates above or below (a plate, a tile, two plates) is a stride of two studs, from the stud before its edge to the stud past it, the leading leg lifted higher and the trailing foot up on its toes; anything higher (a brick) is a hop. A part in its way that it does not climb onto, no higher than a brick over its level and no deeper than two studs (a hurdle, a curb, a log), it leaps: from a stud short of where a foot would stand in it to a stud past where both clear it, on its own level, rising as high as its feet need to clear the part's top, in the walk's time for the studs it crosses. So a hurdler is a walk down the lane, never keyed jumps (a walk's own steps set its height and legs, over any keys or path). A walk not following the ground walks at the height its keys give it as it starts, whatever they key later: walking off a mat onto the floor takes `ground()`. Anything else that moves it over studs leaves it clicked in where it stands until where it is keyed lies a stud on (or a quarter round), and then it hops (or turns) there; in a crowd, a stud straight ahead is a step. Its idling and the boil only tilt it a little, never off its studs. Off studs (on tiles, a slope) its feet still make every move: a walk takes the same steps and turns along the path itself; a crowd's walker steps a stud at a time toward where its crowd puts it, turning on a foot where its way turns; a figure standing turns on a foot once its keys turn it more than 20° (and the rest once they settle), and steps a stud at a time toward where its keys move it, forward or back (a hop to its side), standing afresh where they put it more than 4 studs off at once. In the air or lying down it goes where it is keyed. A crowd's walker swings its arms against its legs as it steps. The ground under it is read from the studio when the film is staged; before that, the whole floor counts as studded baseplate.

A figure's arms hang a little back where nothing keys them, as a brick film figure stands; a key sets an arm exactly as it says, and a figure cast from the set stands as it was built. A figure is never perfectly still, as in a brick film: wherever none of its walks, idles, paths or crowds moves it for more than half a second, it idles subtly on its own (breathing, a slight sway, now and then a glance), over the pose it stands in, its keys still setting what they name. The stage says which actors are figures, so a figure cast from the set idles too, and a car or a building never does; `a.still()` keeps one exactly as keyed. The timeline shows what the scene writes, so this idling has no band of its own.

A story in more than one place cuts between the build's sets: the street, then `set.at(9, 'Garage')`, the garage. A set never eases into another; every set the scene names is staged when the film is, so the cut draws at once, and an actor goes wherever its keys put it in whichever set stands, so the car that left the street rolls into the garage.

A shot runs from one cut to the next. A move may end exactly where the next shot cuts in: `.at(4.5, { orbit: { angle: 15 } }, 'linear').cut(4.5, { position: ... })` moves to the end of one shot, then cuts. An orbit's angle eases by the numbers written, so 345 to 15 swings back through 180 while −15 to 15 swings through 0; a plan shows which way a swing goes.

### The edit

Every time a scene writes is the scene's own: its keys, its sounds' starts, a seek's time. Its `speed` stretches them on the screen: a quarter as fast shows four film frames for each frame of the scene, every key, path and wobble read between the scene's frames, and the film runs longer than the scene (`describeFilm` gives both, `seconds` and `filmSeconds`); the timeline's ruler is the film's own time. A sound starts where its moment shows and plays at its own pace, however fast the scene runs. A film runs at most 7,200 frames at its speed.

A transition is the film's camera's: over its seconds the shot the cut leaves still shows over the one it enters, through the camera as its keys before the cut place it (holding its last place as the world moves on, kept out of the parts as its last frame was); a value first keyed at the cut, such as its lens, holds before that key, so the shot left keeps it as the frames before the cut showed it. Across a set change it is the street as the last frame before the cut showed it, held. A later cut ends a transition still running, with or without one of its own; a cut at the film's start has nothing to leave. A view cuts plainly.

Depth of field is the scene's: once a camera key names an aperture, every look draws it, Draft and Plastic included, so the shallow look shows as the film is made; with none, Photo and Path traced draw a toy photo's f/8 and the looks below are sharp throughout. A shallow lens wants its focus said: on whom the shot is about (`focus: driver`, `{ actor: driver, height: 10 }` for a face), and a rack focus moves it, `.at(4, { focus: cat }, 'hold').at(4.6, { focus: car }, 'ease-in-out')` pulling from the cat to the car over 0.6 s. A focus given as studs ahead stays that far ahead however the camera is kept out of parts.

A roll, like every keyed value, holds before its first key: key `roll: 0` where the camera stands level before a later roll, and `shift: 0` where the lens stands unshifted before a later shift. A shift moves the picture, not where the camera looks: its target stands off the picture's middle by the shift (`screenOf` says where), and it stays in focus. A dolly zoom draws the camera along its line to its target as the lens narrows (and back as it widens), from the lens key before the run of `dollyZoom` keys, so the target keeps its size while the world behind it stretches or flattens; after the run the camera stays where the run left it, and a later run draws on from there, until a key places the camera again. Only a fade to black lowers the film's sound; a flash or a fade to white leaves it.


## The Screen

Each time the scene runs, Stop Motion turns it into a reel, every frame of the film as data, and loads it into Brick Studio once. From then on the timeline only names a frame and the studio shows it: playing, scrubbing and seeking all happen in the studio, the fade and the titles drawn there too. The film plays in the studio while the timeline is scrolled out of view.

The studio holding a build is the main one: its build holds the sets, and its camera is the film's. A Brick Studio with no build of its own is another screen, showing the same set through the scene's next `view`: give the scene a plan view, and a second studio is the film's map. When two studios both hold a build, the film waits until one of them is emptied.

Brick Studio plays the film full screen: the button on its strip fills the screen with the studio, and the strip then carries the film's controls (play and pause, a scrub bar and the film's time; Space and the arrow keys besides), each press asked of Stop Motion's own transport, so the timeline and the studio stay one film. While the film plays the strip rests out of sight once the pointer stands still off it; moving it brings the strip back, and Esc or the button leaves.

The strip under the timeline is the film's picture track: a small picture of each frame, taken from the studio while the film is neither playing nor exporting, without moving the picture the studio shows. **Storyboard** turns it into a cell per camera shot: the shot's middle frame, captioned with its name and span; a press seeks to the shot's start, and the shot the playhead is in stays marked while the film plays. With more shots than fit, the track scrolls. The choice is kept in your browser.

**Export MP4** shows every frame in the studio at the scene's size, captures it with its fade and titles, and encodes the film as H.264 in an MP4 for the browser to save. Changing the scene during an export stops it.

While the film is shown, Brick Studio says so and offers a way back to its build. Pressing it pauses the film at once; **Stage again** shows the film in the studio once more. Nothing in a film is written to the build.


## The File

A film is saved to your Drive as one file: the main studio's build (the set), then the scene, carried in the same LDraw file as a data section (`0 !DATA scene.js`). Brick Studio opens that file as an ordinary design, and keeps the scene in it when it saves, so a film saved from either is whole.

- **Save** saves the set and the scene together. The first save asks for a name and a folder; after that, Save writes over the film's own file, the one it was saved as or opened from, wherever it is.
- **Save as…** saves the film under another name, in the folder you choose, as a new file that is the film's own from then on: Save writes that one. The file saved before is left as it was, so a Charm run again in the same document can keep its earlier film. A name another film holds there asks before replacing it.
- **Open…** opens a film: its set into the studio and its scene into the code block, and the film plays on the new set. A plain design opens its set alone and keeps the scene.
- **New** clears the set (its ground kept) and starts a new scene.

The scene is written into the code editor's tab named `scene.js` as it stands then (a tab renamed from it is left alone; with none, a new one). With no code editor, Open and New change nothing. If the scene cannot be written once the set has changed, Stop Motion says so and the film shows unsaved changes.

The bar says the film's name and whether it has changed since it was saved or opened. New and Open read the film as it stands just before changing it and ask first rather than lose unsaved changes: a film the document has never saved, or changes since it was saved, each said as it is; a set that cannot be read counts as changed, and an edit made in the studio after that read stops the change with nothing replaced. Save, Save as, Open and New run one at a time, in the order asked; a Drive that does not answer within 30 seconds is refused, so the next one runs. Stop Motion is between the two parts: it reads and writes the studio's build and the code block's scene, and nothing else.


## The Sound

A film's sounds are the person's own files in the Platform, however they came there: uploaded, or made by a Charm (`create_speech` speaks a line, `create_music` composes a piece; each returns the file's id). A scene plays one by its URI, `charmiq://file/<assetId>`, reading it through the Drive; *Add sound* picks one from the Drive's folders and writes its line into the scene at the playhead (`sound.at(1.5, 'charmiq://file/…') // Horn.ogg`). The few sounds Stop Motion's example films play ship with it and play by name; they are the audio files in [`sounds`](charmiq://./sounds/README.md), the folder the manifest declares as `sound/` (a sound added there plays by its file's name, with no other change), each Creative Commons Zero.

Stop Motion plays the film's sound itself as the studio shows its picture: started with the playhead, from wherever it starts, seeks or loops, and waiting the moment the studio takes to show a frame so the two meet on screen; at the film's end or its loop the last sounds play out, and a pause stops them at once. The film's fade fades its sound too. A sound following an actor sounds from where the actor stands in the camera's picture, however the camera tilts. A browser lets an Application sound only once the person has pressed in it: a film played otherwise (a Charm's `play`) stays silent, and a *Sound off · click to hear* chip lets it in. A sound whose file cannot be read is left out and a *Sounds missing* chip names it; with none readable, a *No sound* chip says why.

Exported, the film carries the same mix as AAC in its MP4, without any sound that could not be read (the export button names them). A browser that encodes no AAC exports the picture alone, and the export button says so.

## The Timeline

A row per track: the camera, each view, each actor, the sets the scene cuts between, the fade and the titles. A key is a diamond, a path a solid band, a wobble, shake or walk a striped band (double-click a walk to shape its gait, see *The Gait mode*), the fade a ramp to black, a title its words. A camera's shots alternate shades from cut to cut, each named, and so do the sets, each named from its key. Where two actors come within 2 studs of each other, a red notch on both their rows marks the moment they come closest, measured only while both are shown. Keys too close to tell apart draw as one diamond with their count on it, and bands sharing a span stack, the row growing to fit, so nothing hides another.

The Sound row draws each sound as its waveform, over the span it plays once read (over and over to its `until`), named by its file, and each figure's footsteps as one band from its first to its last, saying how many; footsteps follow the figure's steps, so that band is never dragged. Drag a sound along the row and it moves to the frame it is let go on: its time is rewritten where its line writes it (its `until` with it), and nothing else in the scene changes. The timeline reads only code for the call, never a comment or a string, and checks the edit before writing it: the scene as edited must play the same sounds with only that one moved, and the editor takes the edit only while the scene still reads as it was measured. A sound whose time the scene writes as an expression (`LATE`, `HOME + 0.2`) stays where it is, its title saying the line to change.

Press an actor's name on the timeline to follow it: the studio draws it as ghosts at each drawing two seconds back and ahead, outlines round each piece and its silhouette, red behind and green ahead, fainter further off, and its path through its hips over the whole film on the set shown, its keys marked, so a walk, a turn or a near miss reads as a shape while it is still whole at the playhead. Scrub and the ghosts move with it; press the studio's Move the view to look round them from any angle; press the name again, or Escape, to let go. The ghosts are for working: a capture, an export, a thumbnail or the path-traced look never shows them.

Drag anywhere else on the ruler or the rows to scrub; a diamond seeks to its key. The rows scroll under the playhead, which stays on the ruler. Hovering shows a dashed line following the pointer, its time on the ruler. Hold ⌘ (Ctrl off a Mac) as you move and the sheet beside the timeline shows that moment instead of the film: the studio's own picture of the frame (the picture track's nearest, developing, until it arrives), its time, frame, shot and set, where the row's actor or camera stands then, and every mark under the pointer with what it sets, lit on the lane. Let go and the film's sheet comes back. ⌘-click (Ctrl-click) pins the moment in the sheet, marked on the ruler, until Unpin or Escape lets it go; a plain click still scrubs. A key focused from the keyboard shows its moment too. With the timeline focused, the arrows step a frame (with Shift, a second), Home and End go to the ends, and Space plays.

The timeline opens with the whole film across it, and zooms in from there (never further out): pinch on a trackpad, or hold ⌥ (Alt) and turn the wheel, and the moment under the pointer stays under it; with the timeline focused, `=` and `-` zoom about the playhead and `\` fits the whole film (press it again to return); the ruler's corner holds the same as − / Fit / + buttons. Zoomed in, keys drawn as one diamond with a count draw apart, the rows scroll sideways under their names (a sideways swipe, or Shift with the wheel), and the ruler labels halves, quarters and frames, every label to the same places (`3.5s 4.0s`, frames to the millisecond). While the film plays, the view turns a page as the playhead leaves it. The picture track under the timeline always shows the whole film, a yellow bracket framing the span the timeline shows: drag its grip along the top to pan, drag either end to zoom; the pictures under it still seek when pressed.

Opening a film says so: the film picked in the Open dialog shows *Opening…* while everything else in the dialog waits, and the bar says *opening…* until the film is staged. Staging shows its progress in the status, loading the film into the studio frame by frame.

The timeline is a surface notes are made on: with a Review in the document, starting a note marks the moment the playhead is on, and while the note is written a drag across the rows marks a span of frames over the rows it crosses, drawn as an azure band, in place of scrubbing (the ruler still scrubs). Each mark says what it points at: its frames and times, the shot it starts in, and each row, an actor's with what moves it as the span starts (its walk, a path or its keys) and the scene's lines that do, as `describeFilm`'s footing names them. Opening a note loops its spans with their rows lit; a note drawn on the picture alone goes to its frame. Without a Review the timeline scrubs as ever.

## The Gait mode

**Gait** (or double-clicking an actor's walk on the timeline) puts the gait its walker walks in the timeline's place, while the studio keeps showing the film; **Back to the film** (or **Close gait**) returns. A tab for each kind of move a gait keys (Walk, Step up, Step down, Turn, Shift, Crouch, Jump, Land) shows its graphs: the body's place along the move, each leg's and arm's swing, the head, the hips, a key at each of the move's drawings, and sliders for the rest (how many drawings the move takes against a step's, how far the head leads, how high a hop lifts). Drag a dot and the graphs redraw at once; let go and the gait is written into the scene as `gait(name, { ... })`, only what it changes from the built-in walk, each number to two places, in place where the scene already names that gait or as a line of its own at the scene's start, and the film is staged again walking it. Keys the scene writes as anything but `{ ... }` stay as written, the note saying the line to change.

The move the frame shows is marked on its tab and its graphs. The scrubber under the graphs moves through the film (the arrow keys a frame, with Shift a second), and **Loop this move** plays the move under the playhead over and over until pressed again. A line over the tabs says whether the walker's feet stay out of the set: where a foot reaches into it, on how many frames, in which moves and how far at most, and a press shows the worst frame. A foot into the set is the walker's to fix or to live with, by its gait or its path.


## The Commands

A Charm writes the scene in the code editor's `scene.js` tab, then works the film through these:

| Command | Does |
|---|---|
| `describeFilm` | asked nothing, a summary: the studios and editors found, whether the scene is staged, and the film's facts (title, seconds, fps, frames, actors, views, its shots each with the set it starts on, its set keys, its sounds, each with its own length in `seconds` and, played once, the scene's time it `ends` (past the film's `seconds`, it is cut at the film's end), and its footsteps, each walk taking fewer than three steps between two of its leaps over low parts (`leaps`: the walk's `line`, when each leap sets off, and `stepsBetween`, its laid steps between each and the next), where an avoiding camera was drawn in, its log, and once staged the `extents` of the sets it stands on, the area in whole studs each set's parts stand over), or the scene's mistake and its line, the sounds Stop Motion ships, and once staged every problem in a line (`problems`: its kind, its actor or actors, its span in seconds, how much at most with its `unit`, studs or degrees, and its scene lines), the worst first within each kind, with a count of each kind (`counts`, 0 for none). A shot's camera problems of one kind and one cause (the same actor, the same part, or parts of one component, as a stand's figures sweeping past the camera a frame or a few at a time) share one line: `by` the actor, the part, or the component by its name and place (`stand-full at x 38 z -15`), its `shot`, the span from the first to the last, how many `moments` it holds and the `time` its worst (the longest) starts; `counts` counts each moment, and asked `kinds` or a span, `camera` lists each in full. The kinds: `overlap`, two actors standing in each other more than half a stud; `inside` and `hidden`, the camera inside a part or an actor, or one between it and its target (the actor standing at a point the camera aims at, the nearest within its own height of it, or one riding with it, before that point is the shot's own subject: counted apart as `own`, never a problem, each moment in `camera` asked `kinds: ['own']`); `into`, `above`, `floating`, `tipped` and `through`, a figure's feet in the set, out over nothing as it steps, a second in the air as it stands or sits (a figure flying, rising or falling fast as a jump does, never floats), tipped past 45° as it moves (held back from a channel's later first key: `heldBack`, its `keys` with their times and lines and the `fix`, the channel keyed 0 at its last moment tipped), or its body in a part while in the air; and `short`, a keyed flight not clearing a part it crosses. Asked `actors`, a span (`from`, `to`) or `kinds`, the full entries of the problems naming those actors, over that span, of those kinds: `overlaps` (where each stands), `camera`, `footing` (where the figure stands, `at`, its height counted from the baseplate; `surfaceTop`, the top in plates of what its feet are in or over; `aboveSurface`, how many plates over that top it stands, below 0 in it; `on`, the part the ground stands it on; and what places the feet then: its walk, a path or the height keys it eases between, with their lines), `clearances` (every keyed flight over each part it crosses, its least `margin` in studs, below 0 through it, the `time` it is least and the body's `point` setting it then, as `short` names them too, and `cleared`: a fact, short or not), `leaps` (every walk leaping low parts), and, only when `kinds` names `close`, the actors that come within 2 studs (`close`: its threshold, `withinStuds`, and each pair where it comes closest, the nearest first, as the timeline's notches measure them) |
| `seek` | shows a moment (`time` in seconds, or `frame` from 0) in the studios, answering once the studio has drawn it, so a screenshot sees it |
| `play`, `pause` | the transport |
| `storyboard` | shows up to 12 moments at once on a sheet over the main studio, each captioned with its time and shot, through a `view` when named: a screenshot of the studio reads them all. The moments are the `times` or `frames` given, or a `shot`'s (named as `describeFilm`'s shots name it): its first frame, two between at a third and two thirds of the way, and its last, one picture of the shot's motion where a `seek` and a screenshot show one moment; one of the three, and the middle of each shot when none is given. The film's next move closes it |
| `measure` | where two actors come closest, over the film or `from` and `to`: the gap between their boxes in studs (each its footprint turned by its yaw and its height; 0 when they touch) and where each stood |
| `motionOf` | one actor's motion over a span (`from`, `to`, seconds): its place and turn at evenly spaced `samples`, its `height` counted from the baseplate and, following the ground, beside it the `ground`, the top the ground stands it on, from which its keys and paths count (a flop over a bar its footprint overlaps counts from the bar's top: its keyed height is `height` less `ground`), and what drives each value over the span (its walk, a path, its keys, or nothing) with the scene lines; a value read before its channel's first key is that key's, held back from its later time (`heldBack`): a figure whose only pitch key is at its landing lies at that pitch all through its run-up |
| `listen` | hears a `sound` a scene can play (a Platform file's URI or a shipped sound's name) as Stop Motion decodes it to play: its length in `seconds`, and the `spans` it sounds over, first to last, each `from` and `to` in seconds with the `pause` after it. A pause is at least `minPause` seconds (0.3 when not given, 0.05 to 10) more than 40 dB quieter than the sound's loudest, its loudness heard in 20 ms windows, so a whisper still sounds. At most 60 spans: a sound holding more is parted at its 59 longest pauses alone, and `capped` says how many it held. To sync a film to narration a Charm cannot hear, it maps the narration's sentences onto the spans in order (a sentence's commas may part it into more than one; the pauses its text marks fall at span ends) and keys its cuts and effects to their times |
| `surfaces` | what stands where on the set standing at `time`, over an area (`x`, `z` its south-west cell, `width` and `depth` in cells, at most 64 by 64): a map north up, each row's cells west to east as runs of like cells, each run its first cell's `x:` and its tops (`-25:b2x8b@6 -22:b2x8b@6/p1x10o@14 -21:b2x8b@6`: a mat, the bar's column at x -22 over it, the mat again), each top lowest first its part's key, `@` and its height in plates, `0` alone the bare baseplate, `-` for none; a key is read off its part's name, its kind then its color (`brick-2x8 blue` is `b2x8b`), the same in every ask, and the `legend` names each, every top of one part and color under one key (two parts of one map whose names key alike take their whole names, `brick-2x4.blue`). Asked no area, the whole set: its `extent` (the area in whole studs its parts stand over) mapped at the least `cell` of studs that fits it within 64 by 64 cells, each cell the tops most of its studs show, the higher of as many. Read it before routing a walk or a flight, or when a note says something is off its place (a race off its track) |
| `where` | every actor's position and turn, the camera and the set standing, at a `time` or `frame`, or with neither at the playhead's frame (`current`) |
| `screenOf` | every actor on the screen at a `time` or `frame`, as the studio's camera draws it: its box across and down as shares of the frame, how far ahead, whether it is in frame; and the camera's problem then |
| `reviewCuts` | every cut as an editor reads it: the frames either side, each side's subject, where it stands on the screen and which way it moves, the camera's turn and each shot's length, and what an editor would flag (crossing the line, a jump across the screen, a jump cut under 30°, a shot under a second, a subject out of frame, the camera inside something or its target hidden) |
| `listFilms` | the films (and designs) in the films folder, each by name and uri |
| `saveFilm` | saves the set and the scene as one film: under `name` in the films folder (a name other than its own file's saves a new file, the film's own from then on, the earlier one left as it was), or over the film's own file wherever it is; another film of that name needs `replace` |
| `openFilm` | opens a film by `uri` or `name`: its set into the studio, its scene into the code block (a plain design, its set alone) |
| `newFilm` | clears the set and starts a new scene |

`openFilm` and `newFilm` refuse while the film has unsaved changes (the document never saved it, or it has changes since it was saved) unless given `discard: true`; save the film first to keep it.

A Charm can also stage a still without a film: Brick Studio's own `stage` and `frame` commands put actors anywhere.

A Charm sees stills, never playback, so the film is also read as it looks. Staging asks Brick Studio's `probeStage` what each frame's camera stands inside and what stands between it and its target, against the cells the set's parts fill (glass, window and door frames and fences hide nothing). `describeFilm` counts and lists where the camera goes wrong, `screenOf` places the actors on the screen, and `reviewCuts` reads each cut. A plan draws the camera's path dashed where it stands inside a part.
